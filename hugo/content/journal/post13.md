## **The suitors ate the fleet**

So. Mushrooms.

The reason we are here is Homer.

At the beginning of the *Odyssey*, Telemachos is Odysseus' son, heir to a great household, and almost powerless inside it.

The suitors have lived at his expense for years. They eat his animals, drink his wine and consume his stores. Penelope has held them off for three years; the fourth is beginning.[^three-years]

Then Telemachos decides to act.

He wants to go to Pylos and Sparta and ask the men who returned from Troy what became of his father.

He needs a ship.

And he no longer has one he can use.

Telemachos explicitly connects the two things: while he was still a child, the suitors wasted his possessions; now he cannot command a ship and crew of his own and must make the journey in somebody else's vessel.[^ship]

They have eaten his ability to act.

A *basileus* needs property, men, reputation, friends, and the ability to call on them.

Telemachos has the name.

What he needs now are people who will answer it.

  src="images/journal/post13_telemachos_political_voyage.png"

Telemachos is not helped by strangers at random. He is Odysseus' son, and Odysseus' friendships still carry obligations.

Homer invokes another rule: the stranger is under Zeus. Odysseus invokes Zeus as protector of guests and suppliants; Eumaeus later says that strangers and beggars come from Zeus.[^zeus]

The suitors violate that rule. They enter Odysseus' house and consume it.

The men Telemachos meets do the opposite. Noemon lends him a ship. Nestor feeds him, houses him and sends him onward with a chariot and his son. Menelaus receives him at Sparta because he remembers depending on the hospitality of others during his own return from Troy.[^sparta]

Food, transport, gifts and protection keep relationships between households alive.

And the rules matter because their failure has consequences.

Eurymachus tells Halitherses to go home and prophesy to his children, lest they suffer ill. Then he promises the old man pain and shame if he keeps encouraging Telemachos. Homer does not need to specify the fate of those children. His audience knows what the destruction of a household means.[^halitherses]

Later, the threat against Mentor makes that meaning explicit: kill the man, seize the property, dispossess the sons and daughters, drive out the wife.[^mentor]

The threat is not hypothetical. The entire war has already shown what armed men do to defeated women. Hector fears Andromache being carried away into slavery; Agamemnon casually offers captured women among the spoils; Briseis is treated as transferable property; and wider Trojan tradition makes Cassandra the clearest horror of all -- dragged from Athena's sanctuary, raped in some accounts, then allotted to Agamemnon as a war prize.[^trojan-women]

Monsters are not monsters because of their shape. Polyphemus rejects the law of Zeus openly. Troy is full of women carried away as prizes; Cassandra is violated at the very sanctuary where she seeks protection.

Hospitality is therefore not quaint etiquette. It is one of the rules separating a guest from a raider, a traveller from an invader, and a man from the monster he may become.

That was true for Homer. Beaumont inherits a world that has learned the lesson repeatedly.

The nineteenth century was brutal: Revolutionary and Napoleonic wars, famine and industrial poverty, revolutions, the American Civil War, imperial wars.[^moral-ground]

Then came 1914.

The Great War turned the century's achievements -- railways, industry, chemistry, finance, mass organisation -- into machinery for killing, blockade, hunger and displacement.

Beaumont survives into 1932 and builds something else: ships, hotels, trains, routes, kitchens. It moves strangers, houses them, feeds them and connects them.

For money, obviously.

But the product is still hospitality.

## **Rapturous Risotto**

The recipe below is the ideal version.

The fresh mushrooms I actually had were brown *champignons de Paris*. The intended dish gives three mushrooms three different jobs.

The cèpes build the base.

The meadow mushrooms go into the risotto.

The brown Paris mushrooms are cooked separately, hard enough to brown properly, then finished with Japanese soja.

{{< image
  src="images/journal/post13_aquarelle_mushroom_prep.png"
  alt="Mushrooms being prepared for Rapturous Risotto"
  class="journal-image"
>}}

### Ingredients

- Baldo risotto rice
- dried cèpes / porcini, around five percent of the dry rice-and-cèpes base
- rosés des prés / meadow mushrooms
- brown champignons de Paris
- onion
- garlic
- olive oil
- Japanese soy sauce
- fresh thyme
- chives
- butter
- Parmesan
- salt
- hot water or light stock

I used Baldo.

James cannot: the variety dates from 1964.[^baldo]

So Baldo belongs to this recipe; his 1932 version gets an earlier Italian risotto rice.

### Cèpes

Clean the dried cèpes lightly in water, then sponge them.

Wet them again with hot liquid infused with thyme and let them soften. Keep that mushroom-and-thyme liquid: it belongs in the risotto.

The point is not simply to restore water to the mushrooms. Their flavour goes into the liquid, and the liquid comes back into the rice.

### The fresh mushrooms

Cut the meadow mushrooms for the body of the risotto.

Keep the brown Paris mushrooms separate.

They have another job.

{{< image
  src="images/journal/post13_aquarelle_browned_mushrooms_blue_flame.png"
  alt="Brown Paris mushrooms cooking over the flame"
  class="journal-image"
>}}

Heat olive oil in a wide pan and cook the Paris mushrooms hard enough to develop real colour.

Do not crowd them.

Do not stew them grey.

Brown them.

Add the garlic toward the end, then a restrained amount of Japanese soja and the chives.

Reserve them.

### The rice

Soften the onion in olive oil and butter.

Add the rice and toast it briefly.

Add the cèpes and the meadow mushrooms, then begin adding the hot thyme-and-cèpe liquid gradually, supplemented with hot water or light stock as required.

{{< image
  src="images/journal/post13_beaumont_multipan.png"
  alt="Several parts of the Rapturous Risotto being prepared simultaneously in the Epona kitchen"
  class="journal-image"
>}}

Keep cooking until the rice is tender but still has structure.

The risotto and the browned mushrooms remain separate almost to the end.

{{< image
  src="images/journal/post13_beaumont_risotto_and_mushrooms.png"
  alt="Risotto and browned mushrooms being prepared separately aboard Epona"
  class="journal-image"
>}}

Finish the rice with butter and Parmesan.

Taste before adding salt. The Parmesan and soja already have opinions on the matter.

Plate the risotto and add the browned Paris mushrooms.

Chives.

Serve immediately.

{{< image
  src="images/journal/post13_photo_finished_rapturous_risotto.png"
  alt="The finished Rapturous Risotto"
  class="journal-image"
>}}

Preferably to somebody whose continued friendship would be useful.

[^three-years]: Homer, *Odyssey* 2.85–128. Antinous says that Penelope has delayed the suitors for three years and that the fourth is under way; they intend to continue consuming Telemachos' livelihood while she resists them.

[^ship]: Homer, *Odyssey* 2.208–223 and 2.309–320. Telemachos asks for a ship and twenty companions. He then connects the suitors' destruction of his property with the fact that he must make the voyage in another man's vessel rather than command ship and oarsmen of his own.

[^zeus]: Homer, *Odyssey* 9.266–271; compare 14.55–58. Odysseus invokes Zeus as protector and avenger of strangers and suppliants; Eumaeus later says that strangers and beggars come from Zeus.

[^sparta]: Homer, *Odyssey* 3–4. Nestor receives Telemachos at Pylos and sends him onward; Menelaus receives him at Sparta and explicitly remembers the hospitality upon which he himself depended during his travels.

[^halitherses]: Homer, *Odyssey* 2.157–207. Halitherses reads Zeus' omen as a warning to the suitors. Eurymachus invokes his children in his response and threatens the old man with suffering if he continues encouraging Telemachos.

[^mentor]: Homer, *Odyssey* 22. The threat against Mentor spells out the destruction of his household: death, confiscation, dispossession of his children and expulsion of his wife.

[^trojan-women]: Homer, *Iliad* 1, 6 and 9 for Briseis, Andromache and captive women as war prizes. Cassandra's violation at Athena's sanctuary and subsequent allocation to Agamemnon belong to the wider Trojan tradition.

[^moral-ground]: Everyone claims the moral high ground. North and South, Britain and France, republics and empires all found languages of law, liberty, civilisation, order or necessity in which to explain themselves. Reality is rarely that clean. Like the Greeks, we know who we are.

[^kikkoman]: Kikkoman Soy Sauce Museum, “History of Kikkoman Soy Sauce.” Kikkoman records overseas exports long before the twentieth century and reproduces the British newspaper advertisement shown above, dated 1932.

[^baldo]: RisoItaliano, “Baldo: scheda varietale.” Baldo is an Arborio × Stirpe 136 variety created at Vercelli in 1964 and entered in the Italian register in 1967.
